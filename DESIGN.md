# Doriterm Design System

The single source of truth for Doriterm's look and behaviour. Tokens live in `packages/ui/src/styles`, the theme engine in `packages/ui/src/theme`, primitives in `packages/ui/src/components`. The showcase (`bun run --filter @doriface/ui showcase`) renders every primitive in every state and every screen below; `bun run --filter @doriface/ui capture` writes `docs/design/`.

## 1. Atmosphere & Identity

A quiet control room at night. Terminals are the content; everything around them is warm graphite chrome that steps back. The signature is **status you can read without colour**: a spinning arc for working, a "Needs you" chip or `!` mark for blocked, a filled dot for done, a ring for idle, the same grammar on desktop, phone and in the tab strip. Done is attention, not history, as in herdr-app: it is a finish this screen has not looked at yet, and showing that pane turns it idle here, while another device that has not looked still shows it (#611). Depth comes from tonal steps and 1 px hairlines, never glass or heavy shadow.

## 2. Color

### Contract (lead decision 1)

The theme input is a herdr-app terminal palette (background, foreground, selection, cursor, ansi0..15, optional status). `resolveTheme(palette)` is a TypeScript port of herdr-app's `resolve()` and yields herdr-app's 26 semantic roles plus ansi0..15, byte-identical for herdr-app's presets (unit test: graphite and paper match all 42 values). Any herdr-app `themes/<name>.json` imports through `parseThemeFile` with herdr-app's validation rules.

`themeVariables(theme)` projects those roles onto shadcn-compatible CSS variables, exposed to Tailwind v4 through `@theme inline` in `tokens.css`:

**Faint text.** Ghostty draws faint (SGR 2) text at `faint-opacity` 0.5. `--term-faint` is that mix of terminal foreground over terminal background, lifted by the AA guard until it reaches 4.5:1 (measured 4.55 Night, 4.52 Day, 4.51 Graphite, 4.54 Paper; `contrast.test.ts`), the same way Ghostty's `minimum-contrast` would.

**AA guard.** Text roles are moved toward the ink colour in 1 % steps until they reach 4.5:1 against every surface they sit on (canvas, surface, chrome, popover, raised); control edges, focus ring and status glyphs until 3:1. Values that already pass are emitted unchanged, so herdr-app themes keep their colours wherever they are accessible. `contrast.test.ts` checks every built-in theme.

### Rules

- One hue, one meaning: the four status hues appear only in status glyphs, chips and section headers (HIG color C1). Destructive reuses blocked's hue because both mean "this needs care"; it always comes with words.
- Status is never colour-only (lead decision 4, HIG color C3): shape plus label everywhere.
- Accent marks at most the focus ring, the switch's on state, links and the sidebar's update dot (a 6 px dot after Settings, with the words in its accessible name); the primary button is solid ink (`--primary`), not accent.
- Space avatars (`--avatar-{teal,orange,blue,violet,green,pink}`, initial in `--avatar-ink`) are identity, not state: one strong colour per hue, the same in light and dark, always beside the space's name or carrying it as the label on the rail. They are decorative, so the initial needs no text contrast. A new space takes the least used colour; the choice is kept per space.
- Decision (review T9, kept): Day's accent (`ansi4`) and its done status are both `#2f5fc4`; Night's accent `#9fb4e6` and done `#7fa8f5` are neighbouring blues, and Graphite and Paper carry their own done colour beside their `ansi4` accent. An imported herdr-app theme with no `status` array takes done from `ansi4`, the slot Doriterm reads as accent, so such a theme shares the two hues by construction. The UI therefore never relies on done and accent differing in hue, and Day keeps the shared value. The two never meet in one role: done is always a filled dot, with the word "Done" beside it on chips and section headers and as the dot's accessible name on compact rows and tabs, and accent is only a focus ring, a switch track or a link, never a status or level glyph. The rule above ("one hue, one meaning") reads per role, and both AA tests stay as they are.
- `--dim` fails text contrast by design and is used only for decoration. Every secondary text is `--muted-foreground`.

## 5. Components

Every interactive primitive: hover, pressed, focus-visible (2 px `--ring` outline), disabled (dimmed and legible: a Button keeps its own disabled colours and is never a fade over its label. A filled Button gets a `--selected` fill with a `--foreground` label at 70 %, which measures 5.29:1 or more on every surface in every built-in theme (day, night, graphite, paper) (`--background`, `--surface`, `--chrome`, `--raised`, `--popover`). A ghost Button keeps no fill, draws its label in `--muted-foreground` (4.5:1 or more on those surfaces) where the enabled label is `--foreground` at 85 %, and its icon in `--disabled-glyph`, which measures 3:1 or more on those surfaces. That 3:1 floor caps how far a ghost icon can dim: an enabled one in `--muted-foreground` measures 5.3:1 to 7.3:1, so the disabled one sits about 1.5 to 1.8 times lower in contrast. A loading Button is busy, not unavailable, so it keeps its variant's colours. The other primitives dim the same way, from tokens and never by fading the whole control: a disabled Input, NumberStepper value, menu item and ColorSwatch name draw their text in `--muted-foreground`, 5.29:1 or more on every surface (an Input also takes the `--muted` fill and a `--border` edge); a NumberStepper's minus and plus dim like a ghost Button's icon; a Switch keeps its thumb 5.39:1 from its track, a `--muted` track with a `--muted-foreground` thumb when off and a `--muted-foreground` track with a `--muted` thumb when on, so on and off still read by position and fill. Controls built outside the primitives follow the same rule, never `opacity` on the control or its label (#1243): the sidebar's New space there row uses the ghost Button's inks, enabled and disabled, and a machine header with no terminal to open dims only its icon, to `--muted-foreground` at 72 % from the `--muted-foreground` its name keeps) and, where it can wait, loading; the box never changes size between states (state is colour, opacity or a 0.97 press scale only). Hit areas are at least 24x24 CSS px on fine pointers and 44x44 on coarse pointers (an invisible `::after` extends small controls). Built on Base UI (lead decision 2) restyled with cva + tailwind-merge; icons lucide-react; no emoji; no motion library.

- Arrival is the `.motion-arrive` utility (`base.css`): `@starting-style` opacity 0 plus `--arrive-from`, set per region. It runs only once the workspace has painted with its terminals listed and every kept tab, or the default one, shown (a tab on an added machine waits for machine.list; `useArrivalReady` sets `:root[data-ready]`, and clears it when the workspace unmounts), so neither the first screen nor a remount (a reconnect, coming back from Settings) fades in. A region whose clipping ancestor is the shell root (the sidebar) fades without any transform, so no frame of it can sit outside its box (`layout.e2e.ts` measures transformed boxes against their clipping parent); a tab scales inside the tab strip, which scrolls rather than clips. Hiding the sidebar unmounts it at once (no exit motion), so a quick hide and show starts a fresh fade rather than queueing; nothing waits on the motion. Panes inside a tab's split tree get their own grow-from-edge motion with the split-tree work. Under reduced motion the base rule keeps only the opacity: a cross-fade.

- CSS transitions and Base UI's `data-starting-style`/`data-ending-style`, and for panes that change place the Web Animations API (FLIP: translate for position, clip-path for size and a new pane's reveal, since neither changes the box a terminal measures, so it never re-measures mid-move; a window resize plays no motion); no motion library (lead decision 2). Only transform, clip-path, opacity and colours animate.
- Tab reorder: the dragged tab tracks the pointer (lifted with the overlay shadow); neighbours slide aside by `translate` over `--duration-micro` `--ease-out` and the landing gap carries the hover wash; the drop settles into its slot the same way. Under reduced motion neighbours stay put, a 2 px `--ring` caret marks where the tab lands, and the drop cross-fades.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: spinners stop turning and instead pulse their opacity between 100 % and 65 % over 1.8 s (`[data-slot=spinner]` in `base.css`), so ongoing work stays perceivable; slides become fades; other animations end at once.
- Machine reorder (sidebar and phone Machines list): a press that travels 4 px lifts the row (on touch a 300 ms hold; letting go without moving opens its menu), less is a click that opens the machine. The lifted row tracks the pointer with the overlay shadow; the other rows slide by `translate` over `--duration-micro` `--ease-out` and the landing place carries the hover wash; the drop settles into its slot the same way and never opens the row. Escape, a lost window focus or a cancelled pointer puts it back. Under reduced motion the rows stay put, a 2 px `--ring` line marks where the row lands, and the drop cross-fades. The tab strip's move keys (⌘⇧← / ⌘⇧→, and ⌘⇧↑ / ⌘⇧↓ for the vertical list; Ctrl+Shift+PageUp / PageDown elsewhere) move the focused row, which keeps focus. The order is saved on the core (`doriface` document) and every device follows it.
- Feedback: press acknowledged in the same frame (0.97 scale); loading buttons keep their box; toasts only for outcomes not visible in place.
- Keyboard: herdr-app's bindings (⌘T new tab, ⌘B sidebar, ⌘⇧P commands, ⌘, settings, ⌘[ / ⌘] agent prev/next); standard shortcuts keep their meaning (HIG keyboards). ⌘⇧← / ⌘⇧→ move the tab (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+PageUp / PageDown elsewhere, since herdr-app's spaces take Ghostty's `move_tab` chord there), as do the tab's menu and a drag. ⌘⇧. (Ctrl+Shift+. elsewhere) moves focus from a terminal to the sidebar and back, showing a hidden sidebar first, so Tab and Esc stay with the program in the terminal; it does nothing while a modal dialog is open. Every herdr-app default and what Doriterm does with it is in `docs/keymap-herdr-app.md`, generated from herdr-app's vendored key files (`apps/web/src/keys/herdr-app/`, commit recorded there) by the same comparison `herdr-app-parity.test.ts` holds; a herdr-app default Doriterm binds otherwise is listed with its reason in `herdr-app-parity.ts` (#702). A binding Doriterm adds on its own must be free in both upstream keymaps and in the OS and browsers (`upstream-defaults.test.ts` checks it against Ghostty's and herdr-app's defaults and `platform-reserved.ts`): ⌘⇧S is macOS Save As and the Firefox and Edge screenshot.

### Tabs and panes keymap

Tabs belong to a space, as in herdr-app (#404): the sidebar lists the spaces of the core's `herdr-app` documents, the tab bar shows the selected space's tabs in their saved order, "+" adds a tab right after the selected one, and picking a space brings back the tab it showed last. A live terminal that no tab shows is adopted on load into the space whose folder holds it, else into its machine's Home space. A tab holds a split tree: rows and columns nested to any depth, kept in that document so a herdr-app window and every Doriterm device show the same spaces, tabs, order and splits. Focus, zoom, the selected space and each space's tab stay on each device. Where Ghostty (v1.4.0 `Config.zig`) and herdr-app (`defaults/keys*.json`) disagree herdr-app wins (#702), and off macOS no default takes Super, which Windows and GNOME keep: resize there is Ctrl+Alt+Shift+arrows, which older GNOME releases use to move a window between workspaces; the Shell menu and the divider's own keys cover it. Focus and swap take herdr-app's Alt+arrows, Alt+H/J/K/L and Alt+Shift+H/J/K/L there; with no pane that way the key reaches the terminal program, so a lone pane keeps its Meta keys. The OS can still take a key before the page sees it: GNOME switches workspaces with Ctrl+Alt+←/→ by default, and some Windows graphics drivers rotate the screen with Ctrl+Alt+arrows, so there the pane moves need another binding (Settings, Keybindings) or the command palette.

Every action is in the desktop app's Shell menu, which shows the person's own keys (the page reports its keymap to the menu), and in the command palette; the split buttons in the tab bar and in each split pane's header name their keys in the tooltip. With a terminal focused every chord in this table still acts, as herdr-app's global key bindings do before its terminal sees the key (#1922); keys bound to nothing, plain Ctrl+letter among them, reach the program (a Mac's Ctrl+V is herdr-app's Paste image: the clipboard's image as a file, else ^V; keys typed while the clipboard is read reach the program after that ^V, but an image's path comes once its upload ends, after keys typed meanwhile, and once the clipboard has not answered within 1.5 s (a browser asking the person to allow the paste) keys typed meanwhile go at once, a notice says it is waiting, and its answer still counts when it comes: the image's path then, or ^V then if it holds no image or the paste is not allowed, so one Ctrl+V sends the path or ^V, never both, and never drops an allowed image; elsewhere Ctrl+V is the browser's paste, as before), as does a chord with nothing to act on (focus with no pane that way). A program that wants a space number chord's bytes (Ctrl and a digit on macOS, Alt and a digit elsewhere) has the usual chords for them (Ctrl+Space for NUL, Esc or Ctrl+[ for ESC, Ctrl+\ for ^\, Ctrl+] for ^], Ctrl+^ for ^^, Ctrl+_ or Ctrl+/ for ^_, Backspace for DEL, which is what Ctrl+2-8 send; Esc then the digit for a Meta digit), or the person rebinds Go to space N in Settings ▸ Keybindings.

In a browser tab the browser keeps some of these for itself: ⌘T, ⌘W, ⌘⇧W, ⌘N, Ctrl+Shift+N, ⌘1-9, Ctrl+1-9 on Windows and Linux, Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+PgUp/PgDn always, and ⌘⌥←/→ in Chrome and Edge on macOS (previous and next browser tab). There the tab bar's buttons, the pane header buttons, a click on a pane and the command palette do the same, and the installed app (PWA) and the desktop app get every key. ⌘D is the browser's bookmark key and the page takes it, as Ghostty's web builds do.

## 7. Depth & Surface

Strategy: **tonal shift + hairline borders** (lead decision 5). Surfaces step canvas -> surface -> chrome -> raised -> popover; regions are divided by 1 px `--border`. Overlays (menu, popover, dialog, sheet, palette, toast) get the one shadow level `--overlay-shadow`. No glass, no blur, no gradients. Text in an overlay wraps anywhere (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`) and keeps Korean words whole (`word-break: keep-all`), and an overlay is never wider than the viewport (an anchored one than the room beside its anchor), so an unbroken name wraps instead of running off screen.

| Radius       | Value | Usage                      |
| ------------ | ----- | -------------------------- |
| `rounded-xs` | 4 px  | kbd, chips, badges         |
| `rounded-sm` | 6 px  | tooltip, segment, tabs     |
| `rounded-md` | 8 px  | buttons, inputs, rows      |
| `rounded-lg` | 10 px | panes, overlays, cards     |
| `rounded-xl` | 14 px | bottom sheet, board frames |

Nested corners are concentric: segments (6) inside the 8 px track with 2 px padding.

## 9. Personas, Journeys, Ideal State

Personas: (1) **Supervisor** - a developer running several agents across machines from a laptop; (2) **On the move** - the same person on a phone, unblocking one agent; (3) **Keyboard native** - never leaves the keyboard.

Journeys: (1) notice "Needs you" in the sidebar or status bar -> focus the pane -> answer -> the agent moves to Working. (2) Phone: open the agents list -> Needs you is first -> terminal -> tap ctrl + arrows / type -> back. (3) ⌘⇧P -> type "codex" -> Enter.
